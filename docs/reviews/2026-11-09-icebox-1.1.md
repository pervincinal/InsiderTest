## 1. Replay playback (1.1-R)

### (a) Player value

After a loss the player knows *that* they lost, but usually not *why*. On a 90 s match the turning point (a front tower frozen at 25 while the enemy's L3 out-rated it, or a stream left open into a 0/1 standoff) happens while the player is looking at a different part of the map. Watching the match again at 4× takes 15–25 s and shows the whole board, with every ribbon visible. That is pillar 1, **Readable**, applied to the player's own mistake. It also feeds pillar 4, **Short & replayable**: "I see it now" turns straight into RETRY. A shared link lets a friend watch the match itself rather than a still image of it. This is the first social loop that needs no server. It addresses the audit's Retention 1/2 ("the 'almost made it' signal is bot-only") and Peak moment 1/2 (a peak is easier to notice when you can watch it again and send it to someone).

### (b) Smallest shippable version

**Where it lives.**
- **Result card:** a secondary button **WATCH** (AZ "İZLƏ", RU "СМОТРЕТЬ", TR "İZLE") on the row with SHARE, on wins and on defeats. Do not label it "Replay": in the campaign that word already means a retry (ECONOMY §2.1 "replay" = play again). It is not drawn when (i) the match used a Continue (SIM-R1 v1: `continued` records do not verify) or (ii) the record does not verify on this device (see Verify below). It is also not drawn on the tutorial levels 1–4 for a `bare` save (AUD-U9 progressive disclosure), so the first win card stays simple.
- **Shared link:** the share text (`shareText`, `src/ui/share.ts`) gains a third line: `Watch: https://pervincinal.github.io/InsiderTest/#r=<code>`. The record goes in the URL **fragment**. Browsers never send the fragment to the server, so the privacy sentence "nothing is uploaded" stays true. Opening the link starts the web build straight into playback. Native app users who tap the link get the browser too; universal links / app links are a later item. The PNG card itself is unchanged.
- Nothing is added to the title, the level map or the save.

**Playback screen.** This is the play screen in read-only mode:
- The lesson card is skipped and the sim starts at t = 0.
- A small **REPLAY** chip replaces the level chip in the top band (level name under it).
- The bottom band (where the booster bar sits) becomes the controls row: **Pause / Play**, **Speed** (one button cycling **1× → 2× → 4×**, starting at **2×**), **Restart** (from t = 0), **Exit**.
- A thin, **non-interactive** progress line runs across the top of the bottom band (sim time ÷ recorded `timeMs`).
- Map input is off: taps on towers do nothing (no selection, no guide lines, no hints).
- Off: tutorial, stalemate hint, stalemate card (it is UI bookkeeping, GDD §2.4), interstitials, haptics. Audio follows the player's settings.
- Kept, because they are part of what the player watches: the AUD-A10 intent glyphs, the capture effects and the 600 ms win / loss beat (AUD-F4).
- Skins are the **viewer's** (cosmetic, ECONOMY §3.3).

**No scrub bar in 1.1.** Seeking needs keyframes (a snapshot every 10 s, re-simulated forward). That is cheap given `snapshot.ts` and is the first 1.2 item. For 1.1, Restart plus 4× covers "show me again" (a 90 s match replays in 22 s).

**End of playback.** The **result card in replay mode** shows the recorded outcome with stars (level clocks applied to the recorded `timeMs`), the time and the level name. A record closed while `playing` (a Retreat from the stalemate card) ends on the defeat layout titled "Retreated". There are **no** gold / crystal lines, no ×2 gold, no Continue, no Skip, no NEXT and no interstitial. Buttons: **WATCH AGAIN**, **PLAY LEVEL** (only for a campaign level the viewer has unlocked; it starts a normal match with the viewer's own seed and upgrades), **SHARE** (only for one's own match, the same chain as today) and **MENU**. Watching writes nothing: no stars, no defeat counter, no `diagnostics` level_end row.

**Keeping the record.** The last match's record lives **in memory** (`PlayScreen.replayLog`, SIM-R1). WATCH is available from that result card until the next match starts or the app closes. There is no library and no localStorage key in 1.1 (open question 3).

**Verify before play.** Before showing WATCH or starting a shared link, the client runs `verifyReplay` headlessly. That is ≤ 3 600 ticks with no AI, because the record carries the bots' commands; it should take tens of ms on a phone and is measured in 1.1-R.
- `rulesMatch` false, or `matches` false → a card "This match was recorded on another version of Tower Clash" and MENU, no playback.
- A malformed or truncated code → the same card, worded "This link is damaged".
- The playback screen then runs the same record through the normal loop, applying the record's commands in record order from the `beforeTick` hook (§1c). That is the same order as `runReplay`, so what you see is what was verified.

**Payload size.** The SIM-R1 record stores **every** applied command, AI included. The engineer chose this so a record survives AI tuning, which matters for a link that outlives an app update. The source comment estimates "≈ 10–20 kB of JSON" for 60 s; `tests/sim/replayRecord.test.ts`, named there, is not in the tree yet. I measured it in scratch with SIM-R1's own `newReplay` / `recordInto` / `serializeReplay` over all 50 levels × {reference player, naive 4 s line} × 2 seeds; 200/200 records verify:

| per 60 s of sim | median, naive pace (≈ 16 player cmds/min) | median, reference pace (≈ 63/min) | p95 (naive / ref) | max seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| commands (all owners) | 50 | 110 | 99 / 198 | 259 |
| JSON (`serializeReplay`) | **4.1 kB** | **8.8 kB** | 8.3 / 15.9 kB | 21 kB |
| gzip-9 | 0.53 kB | 0.99 kB | 0.79 / 1.60 kB | 1.85 kB |
| deflate-raw + base64url (the `#r=` code) | **≈ 0.7 k chars** | **≈ 1.3 k chars** | 1.0 / 2.0 k | 2.4 k |

So **a 60 s replay is ≈ 4–9 kB as JSON** (SIM-R1's 10–20 kB is the p95–max band) and **≈ 0.7–1.3 k characters as a link code**. The largest whole match measured is 1.8 k characters. The code is `base64url(deflate-raw(serializeReplay(r)))` via the browser's `CompressionStream('deflate-raw')` (iOS 16.4+, Chrome 103+; a lazy fallback is not needed for our floor). **Cap: no link when the code exceeds 6 000 characters** (≈ 3 min at the reference pace). The share falls back to today's text without the Watch line. Player-only commands would be 5–10× smaller (≈ 120–390 characters per 60 s, measured), but that ties the link to one AI build, which is the trade SIM-R1 already declined. Keep it.

### (c) Cost

- **Sim:** no rule change; SIM-R1 ships the record. Two determinism notes go to SIM-R1:
  - **(1) Cross-engine floats.** The sim uses `Math.hypot` in 5 places (`step.ts` 148 / 420, `geometry.ts` 28 / 161, `create.ts` 70). The spec does not require it to be correctly rounded, so V8 (Android, Chromium) and JavaScriptCore (iOS) may differ in the last bit. A lane length that rounds differently can move one arrival by one tick, and a link recorded on an iPhone would then fail to verify on Android. `Math.sqrt(dx*dx + dy*dy)` is IEEE-exact on every engine. Do not change it blind: add a cross-engine e2e (below) and switch only if it fails. The switch is a sim edit with a playtest re-run.
  - **(2) Continue.** v1 does not record a Continue. 1.1-R hides WATCH for continued matches. A "rewind to t + reinforcements" entry is a 1.2 item.
- **Codec:** `encodeReplayLink` / `decodeReplayLink` (deflate-raw + base64url, size cap, error → card) in the lazy chunk.
- **Screens / UI:**
  - the playback mode of `PlayScreen` (`opts.replay`, input off, `runAi` returns the record's entries at their `t` instead of the bots');
  - the controls row;
  - the result card's replay variant (`RESULT.replay.*` rects);
  - the WATCH button;
  - the "another version" / "damaged link" card;
  - `#r=` boot handling in `main.ts` (read `location.hash` once, then `history.replaceState` to clear it so a reload does not replay);
  - strings × 4 locales (≈ 10 keys).
- **Bundle:** eager ≈ +100 B (the `#r=` check that lazy-imports the chunk; WATCH sits in the already-lazy result chunk). New lazy chunk **`replay`** ≈ 2.5 kB gzip (controller, codec, controls, card). This fits inside today's ≈ 1.4 kB eager headroom after PERF-12; measure on landing.
- **e2e** `replay.spec.ts`:
  - win level 1 by autoplay → WATCH → 4× → the end card shows the same stars and time;
  - open the shared `#r=` link in a fresh context → plays → card;
  - a tampered code shows the damaged card;
  - a `rules` mismatch shows the version card;
  - the save is byte-identical before and after watching;
  - **cross-engine:** a code recorded in the Chromium project verifies in the WebKit project.

  Plus `touchTargets` for the controls row and `lazy.spec` for the new chunk.

### (d) Risks, and what the human playtest should tell us first

- **Nobody presses WATCH.** First question for the playtest: after a defeat, does the tester tap WATCH unprompted? After watching, can they say in one sentence why they lost? If neither happens in ≥ 3 of 5 testers, the button moves to the defeat tip (FE-4: "See where it turned → WATCH").
- **4× is unreadable.** Ask whether streams and numbers can be followed at 2× and at 4× on a 360-wide phone. If 4× is mush, the cycle becomes 1× / 2× / 3×.
- **The link does not open, or desyncs on the friend's phone.** This is the iPhone → Android case. The cross-engine e2e catches the float side. The playtest checks the human side: does the link survive WhatsApp / Telegram / iMessage untruncated at ≈ 1.3 k characters?
- **Spoilers / "solution videos" for the daily.** These are acceptable: there is no leaderboard (§6, §7.6) and the daily pays the same whether or not you watched. Watching never counts as an attempt.
- **Bundle drift.** WATCH must never pull the `replay` chunk eagerly. `lazy.spec` guards this.

### (e) Monetisation

ECONOMY §1 principles 1 and 2 apply as written:
- no ad before, during or after playback (the replay-mode card has no `rv_*` and the interstitial gate does not count it);
- no reward for watching or for sharing (incentivised sharing is also against both stores' guidance);
- nothing to buy.

A later "pro replays" (library, scrub) must stay free; it is a convenience, but it is not worth a store SKU.

## 2. Local 2-player on one screen (1.1-P)

### (a) Player value

Tower Clash is easy to explain across a table: numbers on towers, tap to send. Two people on one phone turns the game into something you hand to someone. That is the strongest word-of-mouth moment a game without a server can have. The AI's known weakness (AUD-A3: past band 2 the bots mostly fight each other, not the player) does not exist when the opponent is a person. The tension pillar (2, **Tense**) gets the purest version: a human punishes greed. Pillar 4 (**Short**) holds, because two humans on a symmetric map with the same rates settle in 1–3 minutes. The design constraint is pillar 1, **Readable**: both players must read every number from their own side of the phone.

### (b) Smallest shippable version

**Entry.** A **2 PLAYERS** button on the title, under PLAY, shown once level 4 is cleared (the player who owns the phone has seen links, growth and surround). It opens a small map picker: 4 duel maps, one tap each, plus BACK. There is no lobby, no names and no settings.

**Seating.** One phone, **portrait**, lying on a table or held between two people. **Blue = bottom = P1** (owner `player`); **Red = top = P2** (owner `enemy1`, colour from the current team-colour set, colour-blind orange included). The map's midline is y = 640 (layout px).

**Maps.** 4 hand-authored **duel maps** (Level Designer), **point-symmetric** about (360, 640). Every tower at (x, y) has a twin at (720 − x, 1280 − y) with the same kind, level and units; owners pair as player ↔ enemy1 and neutral ↔ neutral. The same holds for obstacles and mines. Vocabulary is limited to what a level-4 player knows plus one step each:

| Map | Content |
|---|---|
| D1 | barracks only |
| D2 | walls |
| D3 | a fortress pair |
| D4 | artillery + walls |

There are no tanks, no mines, and the D-maps are not in the campaign, daily or weekly pools. A validator check enforces the symmetry. Every map needs ≥ 4 clear lanes per contested tower (the §2.0b structural rule). No tower may start inside the bottom or top 30 px reach (`BAND_TOWER_REACH`), so it does not sit under a band. The content-fit camera does nothing on these maps (they fill the board, z < 1.04).

**Start.** There is no lesson card. Each band shows a **READY** button facing its player. When both are pressed, a centred **3 · 2 · 1** (1 s per step, drawn twice, once per orientation) starts the sim at t = 0. This replaces the AUD-B11 "first input starts the match": in 2P the first input must not hand one side a head start.

**Who taps what.** Up to **two simultaneous fingers**, one per player. Each new press is assigned to a player at press time, and the assignment holds for the whole gesture even if the finger crosses the midline:
1. A press on a tower owned by blue or red goes to that tower's owner.
2. Otherwise (empty ground, a neutral tower, a band) it goes to the player whose half the press is in. Exception: if that player has no tower selected and the other player has one, the press goes to the other player, so a two-tap link across the midline works.
3. A player with a gesture already in progress ignores a second press. A third finger is ignored.

Each player then gets exactly today's grammar (§2.0b rule 8): tap select / tap target, drag, swipe-to-cut (it cuts only the swiper's own streams), the AUD-U6 400 ms guard and the AUD-U8 release-on-ground cut. Guide lines and hints (`hint.blocked`, the link limit) are drawn in the owner's colour, in the owner's band orientation.

**HUD bands.**

| Band | Layout px | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom (P1) | 1180–1280 | P1's **streams pill**, the **match timer**, **pause** |
| Top (P2) | 0–96 | the same three, **rotated 180°** about the screen centre, so P2 sees exactly P1's layout |

Mute is on P1's side only. **Not drawn:** booster bar, coin pill, level chip (the map name shows on the READY card), speed tag. **Tower badges face the nearer player:** a badge whose tower is at y < 640 is drawn rotated 180°. Because digits seen upside down can be misread (6 / 9, 66 / 99), every badge in 2P gets a **1.5 px baseline bar** under its number (Tech Artist, render-only). Pause from either band pauses the sim. The pause card faces the player who pressed and has RESUME / REMATCH / MENU; either player may tap RESUME.

**AI off.** The level's `enemies[]` still declares `enemy1`, so colours, `state.enemies` and `getOutcome` work unchanged, but no bot runs for it. The intent glyph (AUD-A10) is not shown.

**Rules that change in the UI only (the sim is unchanged).**
- **Modifiers:** both players play `DEFAULT_MODIFIERS`. Commander upgrades never apply, so the phone's owner has no edge. The sim applies `state.modifiers` to `player` only, so passing the default is the whole implementation.
- **Win:** `getOutcome` = `won` → **Blue wins**, `lost` → **Red wins**. One UI rule is added. `getOutcome` checks the player first, so a tick in which both sides end with no tower and no unit would read `lost` (a hidden Red edge). In 2P, when `lost` is returned and red also holds nothing, the match is a **Draw**.
- **Stalemate:** the 45 s card (§2.4) shows at the centre, drawn for both orientations. Choices: **Keep playing** / **Call it a draw**. There is no Retreat-as-defeat; a player who wants to give up can say so.
- **No match cap otherwise**, as in the campaign.
- **Sounds:** the player-POV sounds (the under-attack hit, the lost-tower thud, their haptics) are muted in 2P, because one speaker cannot say whose tower it was. Every capture plays the capture chime and the screen nudge. Music and mute behave as usual.
- **Speed ×2** is off (pause card).

**Result.** Each half shows its own banner facing its player: **VICTORY** / **DEFEAT** / **DRAW**, with the time. A session tally ("Blue 2 – 1 Red", in memory, reset on MENU) sits under the banners. Buttons at the centre: **REMATCH** (same map, same seats), **MAPS**, **MENU**. There are no stars, no gold, no SHARE in 1.1 (later: a duel share card with the tally) and no WATCH (later: 2P replays are free, because the SIM-R1 log already records both human command streams).

**Boosters / gold / stars / crystals: none.** The bar is not drawn, booster hotkeys do nothing, no charge is consumed, no `rv_*` placement is offered, no interstitial fires on leaving the 2P result (the ECONOMY §5.1 counter does not see it), and no achievement is evaluated.

**Why it must NOT touch the campaign save.** `towerclash.save.v3` holds the owner's progress: stars, gold, crystals, streaks, achievements, the defeat streak that gates the skip, and `challenge` / `weekly`. A 2P result is not the owner beating the game. "Blue" may be a friend, and either player may be a child tapping at random. Any write would leak in:
- a "win" for the stars or achievement counters;
- a "loss" for the defeat counter, which feeds the skip valve and the interstitial gate;
- even a harmless counter, which would need a schema field, a `normalizeSave` rule, the AUD-Q2 backup and the AUD-Q4 native mirror.

So **1.1-P writes nothing to localStorage**: no save write, no diagnostics row, no settings change. The e2e proves it byte for byte. The tally lives in memory. A persistent duel history, if wanted, gets its own key in 1.2, never the save.

**Where the second human plugs in (exact).**
1. **`src/sim/types.ts`:** nothing. P2 *is* `enemy1`; `Owner` / `EnemyOwner` stay as they are, `applyCommand` already validates `cmd.owner` against the tower's owner, and `getOutcome` is already the two-sided test.
2. **`src/input/pointer.ts` `attachPointer`:** today one `activeId` (`onDown` returns while a pointer is active). In duel mode this becomes a `Map<pointerId, side>` of at most two entries. The press-time routing above decides each side, and move / up / cancel go to that side's handlers.
3. **`src/input/pointer.ts` `PlayGestures`:** the literal `'player'` (lines 216, 313, 325, 423, 440, 461, 487, 493, 506, 517, 541, 579, 582: link / unlink emission, own-tower tests, swipe-cut ownership) becomes `GestureOptions.owner: Owner`, default `'player'`. In 2P the play screen holds **two** instances (`player`, `enemy1`). Both `onCommand` callbacks go to `PlayScreen.command` → `GameLoop.enqueue`, so both human sources share the one queue applied before the next tick (`loop.ts`). Order inside one tick is irrelevant: a link or unlink only touches its owner's towers.
4. **`src/ui/play.ts` `runAi`** (l. 450): in duel mode no `enemyRngs` entry is made for `enemy1`, so the `for (const enemy of state.enemies)` loop issues nothing (the test is `rng` present). `playerBot` / `loseBot` stay as debug hooks, and a new `setDuelBot(owner, bot)` debug hook lets e2e drive red without multi-touch.
5. **Tutorial, `bare`, lesson card, StalemateWatch hint, intent glyph, booster bar:** off by the `duel` flag in `StartOptions`.

**Fairness check.** A point-symmetric map is fair by construction except for processing order: the sim iterates towers in id order, so ties (two captures in one tick) may lean one way. **AI Engineer gate:** the same enemy personality on both sides (the bot needs an owner parameter for blue), 100 seeds per D-map. Blue's win share must be in **45–55 %**. If it is not, re-id the twins so that the order alternates.

### (d) Risks, and what the human playtest should tell us first

- **Hands cover the battlefield.** Two hands over one 6" screen. First question: do testers hold the phone between them or lay it flat, and does a hand hide the midfield streams? If holding fails, the default seat is "flat on a table" and the store text says so.
- **Upside-down numbers.** Can P2 read badges in P1's half (and the other way round) without asking? Is the baseline bar enough to tell 6 from 9?
- **Cross-touch mistakes.** Count wrong-owner gestures per match: a press that went to the other player, a cut that cut nothing. If more than about 1 per match, drop rule 2's exception and make the press-half the only rule (two-tap links across the midline then need a drag).
- **Stomps.** The owner has 50 levels of practice and the friend has none. If veteran vs newcomer ends in < 45 s, the match is a demo, not a game. The fix is a handicap (a `startGarrisonBonus` for the weaker side), which needs per-owner modifiers in the sim (§7.6 "enemy-side mutators"). That is a sim item for 1.2, not 1.1. 1.1's mitigation is map choice: D1 is slow and has wide neutral rings.
- **Store / platform:** none expected. Local multiplayer is not "multiplayer" in the GDD §6 server sense, but §6's wording needs the 1.1 amendment ("local, one device").

### (e) Monetisation

Same as replay: nothing to buy, no ads in or around a 2P match, no rewards. ECONOMY §1 principle 1 rules out a paid map pack with an advantage. A cosmetic one (a skin applying to both sides) would only make sense if the friend could also buy it, which they cannot on the owner's phone. So: no 2P SKU in 1.1. Revisit only with data that 2P sessions convert friends to installs.

## 3. Recommendation

**Order: replay first (1.1-R), then 2-player (1.1-P).**
- Replay is half the cost, has no input or content risk, and builds on today's SIM-R1. Its record is what makes a 2P replay or a bug report free later.
- 2P needs the multi-touch routing proven on real devices before anyone draws the bands. QA can start the CDP two-finger test while 1.1-R is in review.

**Into 1.1:**
- 1.1-R as in §1b: watch the last match and shared links, 1× / 2× / 4×, no scrub, in-memory last record, verify-before-play, 6 000-character link cap.
- 1.1-P as in §2b: 4 D-maps, two thumbs, no boosters / gold / stars / save.

Target as POST_LAUNCH §6.2: the 1.1 tag at L+27; anything whose gate is red slips to 1.2.

**Later (1.2+):**
- scrub with 10 s keyframes;
- a Continue entry in the record (so continued matches can be watched);
- a small replay library (last 5, a separate key, never the save);
